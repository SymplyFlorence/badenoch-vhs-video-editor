Robert Jenrick joins Reform UK in chaotic press conference after being sacked from Tories

  • Robert Jenrick
  • Conservatives
  • Politics
  • Thursday 15 January 2026 at 10:31pm

Robert Jenrick has become the latest in a long line of Tory politicians to defect to Reform UK, as ITV News' Political Correspondent Shehab Khan reports

Robert Jenrick has defected to Reform UK, Nigel Farage announced at a chaotic press conference, where the former shadow justice secretary was absent after being called to the stage.

When Jenrick - who was earlier sacked from the Tories - ran in several minutes later, he launched a scathing attack on his former Conservative colleagues, listing their individual shortfalls one by one.

It comes after Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch announced she had fired him on Thursday due to “irrefutable evidence that he was plotting in secret to defect” from the party in a manner as "damaging as possible” to the Tories.

Farage, who had teased earlier in the day that he'd give Jenrick "a ring this afternoon" after the news broke, told the press briefing that Badenoch had today gifted him "the latest Christmas present I've ever had" and that Jenrick's move would help "realign the centre right of British politics".

But when the moment came to welcome his latest defectee, Jenrick was nowhere to be seen for several minutes, leaving Farage alone on the stage.

A Tory source said Jenrick was late to the stage because he was rewriting his speech to remove parts the Conservatives had released to the media.

Jenrick, who has become the latest in a long line of Tory politicians to defect, said he'd joined Reform because the Conservatives “don’t have the stomach for the radical change this country needs”.

He said: “I can’t kid myself anymore. The party hasn’t changed, and it won’t.

“The bulk of the party don’t get it. Don’t have the stomach for the radical change this country needs.”

Farage said he had been speaking with Jenrick for "some weeks, some months".

"As indeed, I've been talking to many, many other senior Conservative figures, and incidentally some Labour ones as well. There'll be a Labour defection next week," he said.

Conservative Leader Kemi Badenoch announced earlier that Jenrick had been sacked

Earlier, Badenoch announced in a post on X: “I have sacked Robert Jenrick from the shadow cabinet, removed the whip and suspended his party membership with immediate effect.

“I was presented with clear, irrefutable evidence that he was plotting in secret to defect in a way designed to be as damaging as possible to his shadow cabinet colleagues and the wider Conservative Party.

“The British public are tired of political psychodrama and so am I. They saw too much of it in the last government, they’re seeing too much of it in THIS government. I will not repeat those mistakes.”

Former Conservative politicians are defecting to Reform because 'they know the Tory party is a sinking ship,' Starmer said

However, Farage had denied there was a "plot" for Jenrick to defect to Reform UK this afternoon.

Answering a question from ITV News' Peter Smith at a press conference earlier on Thursday, he said: "What I'm telling you, and what I'm telling Kemi if she's watching, is there's no deal been signed. Conversations have been had, ideas have been kicked around the room, but no deal has been signed."

Farage told ITV News' Peter Smith in Edinburgh that no deal had been signed to secure a Jenrick move to Reform

Badenoch announced later on Thursday that she had appointed Nick Timothy as Jenrick's replacement.

She said that Timothy, who previously backed Jenrick's campaign for Conservative leadership in 2024, is "a true Conservative, brings a wealth of experience, and is a formidable campaigner".

Jenrick has attracted speculation about leadership ambitions since being beaten in his bid to take over the party’s reins in 2024, and has repeatedly stepped out beyond the shadow cabinet line.

A Conservative Party source reiterated that the evidence that Jenrick had been on the cusp of defection was "totally irrefutable".

Is Jenrick’s expulsion good for the Tories?

Starmer: Jenrick 'still running leadership campaign'

The source said: “We have had lots of reports coming in that he was meeting with Reform. He went for dinner with Nigel Farage last month, he had been speaking to people within Westminster about the possibility of a defection.“His team has spoken to various people, including journalists, about defection."

Tory Chief Whip Rebecca Harris informed Jenrick he had been sacked and had the whip and his Conservative Party membership suspended, the source said.

Meanwhile, the prime minister questioned why it took “so long” for Badenoch to sack Jenrick.

Sir Keir Starmer accused Jenrick of making “toxic comments to try and divide our country”.

Speaking to broadcasters during a visit to Scotland, the PM said: “My question is: why did it take so long?

"Jenrick has been making toxic comments to try and divide our country for months, and months, and months, but it’s only now, when he’s on the verge of defecting to Reform, that Badenoch gets around to sacking him. So, that’s weakness on her part.

“There’s a bigger story here, because we’re seeing a flood of Tory politicians, ex-politicians, going across to Reform because they know that the Tory Party is a sinking ship.

“Equally, from Reform’s point of view, you’ve got Nigel Farage who is welcoming these failed politicians into his ranks and building his party as a party of the Tory politicians who let the country down so badly.”
